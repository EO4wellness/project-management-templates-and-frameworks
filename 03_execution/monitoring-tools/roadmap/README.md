# Roadmap Template — README

## Overview
A Roadmap is a high‑level, strategic visualization that communicates the direction of a project, product, or initiative over time. Unlike a Gantt chart, which focuses on task‑level execution, a roadmap highlights **major initiatives, milestones, and outcomes** across quarters or months.

This template provides both **Excel** and **Google Sheets** versions designed for clarity, stakeholder communication, and AI‑ready analysis.

---

## File Contents
- **roadmap-template.xlsx** — Fully formatted Excel roadmap  
- **roadmap-template-google-sheets.md** — Instructions + link for Google Sheets version  
- **/assets/** (optional) — Sample roadmap visuals or color palettes

## How to Use This Template

### 1. Define Your Timeframe
Choose the scale that fits your project:
- Quarterly (Q1–Q4)  
- Monthly  
- Half‑year  
- Yearly  

The template defaults to a **quarterly roadmap**, which works well for most projects.

### 2. Add Your Swim-lanes
Common swim-lane categories include:
- Product  
- Work-stream  
- Team  
- Strategic Pillar  
- Customer Segment  

Swim-lanes help stakeholders quickly understand who owns what.

### 3. Add Initiatives
For each initiative, fill in:
- Initiative Name  
- Owner  
- Start Quarter  
- End Quarter  
- Status  
- Impact Level (Low / Medium / High)  

The template automatically visualizes initiatives as horizontal bars across the timeline.

### 4. Customize Visuals
You can:
- Adjust colors by status  
- Add a “Today” vertical line  
- Insert milestone markers  
- Add labels for major releases or decision points  

### 5. Share With Stakeholders
Roadmaps are communication tools — export them as:
- PDF  
- PNG  
- Google Sheets link  
- Slide deck insert

## Best Practices

### Focus on Outcomes, Not Tasks
Roadmaps should communicate **what will be achieved**, not every step required to achieve it.  
Keep initiative names outcome‑oriented (e.g., “Launch Mobile App v2” instead of “Design UI mockups”).

### Keep It High‑Level
A roadmap is not a project plan.  
Avoid granular details — stick to major initiatives and milestones.

### Use Consistent Time Scales
Mixing months and quarters confuses stakeholders.  
Choose one scale and stick to it.

### Show Status Clearly
Use color coding or icons to indicate:
- On Track  
- At Risk  
- Delayed  
- Completed  

Clear status indicators reduce the need for verbal explanation.

### Use Confidence Ratings for Milestones
Not all deadlines carry the same level of certainty. Confidence ratings help stakeholders understand how likely a milestone is to land on time.

Use a simple scale:
- **High Confidence** — The team has done this type of work many times; requirements are stable; risks are known.
- **Medium Confidence** — Some elements are familiar, but there are unknowns or partial dependencies.
- **Low Confidence** — The work is new, complex, or has never been done by the team; requirements may evolve; risks are high.

Confidence ratings prevent over‑promising and help teams communicate uncertainty without sounding negative.

### Revisit and Update Regularly
Roadmaps are living documents.  
Update them at least once per month or at the end of each sprint/iteration.

## Roadmap Clarity Checklist
Use this quick checklist (inspired by PM best practices) to ensure your roadmap communicates effectively:

- **Is each initiative clearly tied to an outcome?**  
- **Does the roadmap avoid task‑level detail?**  
- **Are swim-lanes meaningful and not overly granular?**  
- **Is the timeline scale consistent?**  
- **Are statuses visually clear?**  
- **Does the roadmap tell a coherent story at a glance?**

If the answer is “no” to any of these, refine before sharing.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Treating the Roadmap as a Commitment
A roadmap is a **forecast**, not a contract.  
Communicate that dates are directional, not promises.

### Overloading the Roadmap
Too many initiatives make the roadmap unreadable.  
If everything is important, nothing is.

### Mixing Strategy and Execution
Roadmaps should show **strategic direction**, not sprint‑level execution.  
Keep execution details in your Gantt or backlog.

### Ignoring Dependencies
Even though roadmaps are high‑level, major dependencies still matter.  
If Initiative B cannot start until Initiative A finishes, show it clearly.

### Not Showing Changes Over Time
Stakeholders need to see how priorities evolve.  
Version your roadmap (v1.0, v1.1, etc.) and document changes.
