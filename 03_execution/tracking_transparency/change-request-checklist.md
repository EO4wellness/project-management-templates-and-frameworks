# Change Request Checklist

A checklist to use before designing, submitting or approving a Change Request:

## Completeness
- CR has a clear, descriptive title  
- Reason for change is documented  
- Impact analysis is thorough and specific  
- Alternatives are listed (even briefly)  
- Risks introduced are identified  
- Dependencies affected are listed  
- Effort estimate is realistic  
- Implementation plan is actionable  

## Decision Readiness
- Approved by- is clearly identified  
- Requested decision date is reasonable  
- CR is linked to related issues, risks, or dependencies  
- CR is written neutrally and factually  
- No missing information that would delay approval  

## Governance
- Change is significant enough to warrant formal change control  
- CR aligns with project governance rules  
- Decision will be logged in the Decision Log  
- Stakeholders impacted by the change are identified  
- **Change has been evaluated for scope creep (explicitly check for hidden or incremental scope expansion)**

## Scope Creep Evaluation
- Does this change add new deliverables or requirements?  
- Does this change expand the scope of existing work?  
- Does this change require additional resources or time?  
- Is this change part of a pattern of incremental scope increases?  
- Has the team acknowledged the scope impact and agreed to adjustments?

# Checklist Design Best Practices

These questions help ensure the checklist is useful, scannable, and aligned with PM best practices.

### 1. What failure am I trying to prevent?
Checklists exist to stop predictable mistakes.  
If the failure mode isn’t clear, the checklist becomes noise.

### 2. Who will use this checklist, and in what context?
PM? Developer? Stakeholder?  
During a meeting? During execution? During review?  
This determines length, tone, and complexity.

### 3. What is the minimum set of steps needed to prevent errors?
Checklists should be short, scannable, and high‑impact.  
If it’s too long, people stop using it.

### 4. What decisions or approvals depend on this checklist?
This ensures the checklist supports governance and isn’t just documentation fluff.

### 5. What information must be present for someone to act confidently?
Prevents “I can’t approve this because something is missing.”

### 6. What is the cost of missing an item on this checklist?
High‑impact items go at the top.  
Low‑impact items may not belong at all.

### 7. Does this checklist reduce ambiguity?
If it doesn’t make the process clearer, it’s not doing its job.

### 8. Is this checklist meant for:
- Verification (Did we do everything?)  
- Preparation (Are we ready?)  
- Decision support (Do we have enough info?)  

Knowing the type prevents mixing incompatible items.

### 9. Can this checklist be completed in under 2 minutes?
If not, it’s a form — not a checklist.

### 10. Does this checklist align with other artifacts?
For example:  
- Change Request ↔ Decision Log  
- Issue Log ↔ Risk Register  
- UAT Checklist ↔ Acceptance Criteria  

This prevents contradictions and duplication.
